# Democracy is dead. Long live democracy.

I think it's really aspirational that politicians and state prosecutors are to represent everyone; themselves, their opponents, and the society they serve at large.

A single, black mother with 4 children living pay-cheque to pay-cheque. A single, white man with an 8 figure salary. Both exist in society and both need to be equally valued and represented. In theory.

But if at the end the verdict comes down to 1 judge, a dozen jurors, or 100 politicians, how is that actually representative of the millions of people who are going to be affected by new law or legal precedent?

## "Power corrupts."
This is something people like to say, and I think there's a nugget of truth of there, but really, we're talking about the ability to do stuff. People will always find a way to do what they wanna do. The question is what tools do they have at their disposal, how will it affect them, and how easy is it.

If, for example, you wanted to get a law passed, you'd have to get a ton of support from ordinary people, maybe collect signatures, invest a bunch of your own time working on a movement. Best case scenario, you get a hearing and the politicians agree to "do something." Or, you know, you just buy yourself some support, take some politicians out for a nice dinner, persuade them a little. Notice how only one of these costs you tons of energy, luck, and years of your life. That's not fair, but not necessarily corrupt.

## "Money is the problem."
Now, I can see how you would get to that conclusion. Certainly in my above example, the rich guy had it a lot easier. But I argue that it's really not, and I also don't wanna touch on money too much, but, briefly: It works for many parts of society as we know it, and we can't simply make a change like "just get rid of it."

## Long live democracy.
Really, it just needs to be more representative. The system seems corrupt because manipulating or buying out the laughably small number of people who make all these decisions that affect an entire society is simply too easy.

But, what can we, the people, actually do? Well, we can get more involved, talk to the people around us, get them involved as well. The people living in a society should be educated about the systems that (are supposed to) uphold that society.

And if it truly doesn't work out and "the people in charge" don't acknowledge the millions they're supposed to represent, then they will find that those people can drop the pretence and show them strength in numbers.
