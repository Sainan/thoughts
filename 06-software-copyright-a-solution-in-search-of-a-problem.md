# Software Copyright: A solution in search of a problem

Imagine this: Your company just had a leak. Every line of code your developers ever wrote is out there for everyone to just download on the internet. And now, as a consequence, well, there's a headline on the news about it, maybe? Yeah, no one cares. The vast majority of people are not even gonna able to appreciate the work that was put into the code, and those who can might think it's interesting, maybe get an idea of how 1 or 2 of your algorithms work, and that'll be it.

Really, the value provided by software developers is convenience. People pay because they just want something that they can use and that works. There are projects on GitHub right now that are also available on storefronts, and I have no doubt that it works. I think I would've still made all the same money if all the code I ever wrote was open-source.

And yet, despite the banality of it, software is actually one of those areas where you have endless debates about "what licence to use" and this draconian law where the mere existence of lines of code on a piece of equipment actually means you don't own what you bought and paid for.

It also runs counter to programming culture; one where people often quite literally copy and paste entire chunks of code. It would be hypocritical if any programmer actually had a problem with this. For me, I try to share as much as possible. I hope even one person finds it interesting to know how I implemented 1 or 2 algorithms.

So, we don't need protection; thank you, lawmakers, but no thank you.
