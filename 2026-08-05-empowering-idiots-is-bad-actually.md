# Empowering idiots is bad, actually. (Re: AI)
Yes, there are many bad things I could say about LLMs and the shady goingons behind them but I will focus on the human aspect of it here.

## Getting answers
Even if there are really good resources on a topic, people are not happy anymore with just a "this is the relevant resource," they want a specific sentence, ideally tailored to them. To be fair, Google already set this kind of expectation, but "chatting" and "googling" could hardly be conflated. With how LLMs are behind chat interfaces, the expectation of how "a chat" should go changes.

## Pushback
And so, the expectations of other humans are affected as well. A "chat" with AI rarely results in pushback, or Goddess forbid: An opposing opinion. I think it's natural to get some kind of bad feeling when you encounter opposing views, but I feel like now when people encounter them in "chats," it seems to almost anger them, as if they are an insult. And with that spark, cue the escalation...

## I can do it myself
But it's okay, "needing other people" is so not in anymore. Everyone can do everything themselves now (with a little help from AI). Getting help from a programmer, commissioning an artist, filing a ticket; these are all obsolete. And it's sad because people not only forgo doing these things which are objectively a net positive for society, they really do believe that they are the ones doing "the real work" (with a little help from AI).

## Bonus: Vibe coding
I revised that last section a lot, trying not to get bogged down too much on "programming specifics," but one point I did write and wanted to include: people don't suddenly understand logical thinking, creating algorithms, or what problems are even solvable; these considerations aren't even relevant, not to the prompter, and not to the LLM. If you ask it to solve an impossible problem, it will write you a heuristic that covers your test cases. Found a failure case? No worries, it can fix that for you without addressing the underlying problem. That's tech debt; but I suppose you can be happy that you increased your codebase's GDP.
